Superposition
Immortality is the ultimate goal of humanity... right?
Fiction and Science Fiction

========================================

Chapter 1
---------

"What do you mean you 'can't recall?' This isn't the kind of thing a person just forgets! Not a right-thinking person in the world would claim as much. Do you really think you can just refuse to answer a legal inquiry, Mr. Rapton?" the unpleasant and harsh matronly voice interrogated, threateningly, "Do you understand that you'll be in jail for the duration of the trial, for contempt?"

"Contempt", Eddie thought to himself. "I'll be in contempt alright. Whether they want to silence you or force you to speak, free speech is a farce." He considered whether he would speak, "Free speech my ass". His mouth opened, and he heard his thought out loud in the third person. "Free speech my ass, your honor". The guards advanced. As they put the cuffs on his wrists, Eddie thought "At least I won't have to find a hotel". 

Eddie Rapton was in jail.

"So what's for dinner around here?", Eddie asked the guard on duty, "And what do I call you?". "My name's Jessop, or Officer Marley if you want. Well my name is Jessop Marley. You decide". the guard answered wearily. He had been spending his days continuously following orders which generally included being harsh to the prisoners. Or at least, that was the command. In reality, he wasn't harsh; he was never harsh. He gave respect where he could and hoped for the same. That, and a nap. "And you're going to have what I'm having; what the entire building is in fact having. It isn't bad".

"He'd become tired of it. He's fatigued." Eddie thought. "Anyway he seems exhausted in some way; I can stay on his good side if I'm easy." Again, Eddie's mouth moved itself, "I'll have what your having, you'll get no trouble for me about it," it said, "Can't be worse than what I was going to have tonight." And he hoped that the convenience store room-temperature sandwich he had planned was in fact worse than what he would be having with Jessop.


Chapter 2
---------

"Here you go." Jessop dropped off a steel partitioned plate that looked like something you'd see in a primary school, with what appeared to be Salisbury steak and corn bits. This was actually the most appealing meal he had seen in a long time. "The Singular government's teeth are dull," he thought. "It doesn't even make its punishment unpleasant". He then considered what that thought said about his own life.

"Thanks Jessop. Do you know how the case is going? Is it almost over?" Eddie wasn't exactly certain of how long he had been in jail at this point, but it was not the first meal they had eaten together in the last months. "I mean, does it look like the judge will give a verdict soon, or do you think it'll get a new jury again?" In his mind, he saw two possibilities. One, the case moves on like normal and a judgment is given. In the second, there never is a trial. "And," he thought silently, "what's the deal with replacing the juries over and over?" He thought he felt an answer behind his question to himself. He did not.

"Well that's hard to say Ed. The whole case was put on a media blockade and we don't really know anything! All we know at this time is that there have already been four juries, and none of them have been allowed to leave yet. It must be getting serious in there." Jessop furrowed his brow, looking concerned about more than just the trial intrigue. "It seems the juries are in the same boat as me, prisoner over someone else's case." Eddie noted in his simple, intelligent manner.

He couldn't let the concern float around in the air between them like a confused fly looking for the window from which it came for long. "I have to know. There's nothing else to ground me to reality. That's the real threat of the jails, you have nothing outside to ground on," he thought, so he asked. "What's the matter Jess? On any other day you would be on about the virtues of the Singularity's justice system and giving me at least something to think on. What's got you? You can tell me, we're friends now right?"

"I wouldn't go that far Ed ... but, we're on a first-name basis, I'll concede that much." Jessop's concerned strain didn't drop from his forehead as he cut a piece off the steak using his fork and ate the somewhat bland but essentially good food. Eddie noticed with unintended attention the little wrinkled smiley face made of dimples and lines on Jessop's forehead as came into clearer relief.

"Alright Ed, what I'm about to say never leaves this room. Got it?" Jessop looked deadly serious, so Eddie thought it best to acquiesce. "I understand, I'll keep silent. I swear I will, just let me in on what's up. I have to know." Jessop sat up straight and grabbed his keys and moved his eyes and head from side to side as in the common gesture of suspicion, expecting ears on the walls and eyes watching from the ceiling; he reminded himself of the blind spot maintained for lawyer-client privilege's sake in the jails, walked over to the cell, unlocked it, came in, and sat down next to Eddie.

"We've got to be quiet, we don't know who's around the corner" Jessop said with a hushed voice and a conspiratorial lean. Eddie screwed his face up and answered "Jess there's no corner to be around. It's a square room with a single door, and the door is all the way over there. I don't think anyone can hear us." Jessop looked like he could slap Eddie for the response, but settled down instead when he considered that Eddie had been right.

"Well here's the thing..." Jessop looked simultaneously over-stimulated and under-slept; wild eyes and hair unkempt in the way it gets when a person hasn't been sleeping well, disheveled and nervous. "I think they might be hiding something. I mean I think they are hiding something. I mean more than just keeping a trial secret; we know about this trial happening even if we don't know anything more than that, even without details we're at least aware of trials like this." he stammered, trying to grab ahold of his thoughts before they could slip out without review. "It seems like every day there are more and more people being brought in for the stupidest little things. Things that nobody thought needed to be illegal, so we don't actually have laws about them. Enforcement, but no laws." Jessop was getting overexcited, or overstimulated by what he was saying. Eddie was trying to follow, and Jessop seemed to evade.

"It's just... Well, Ed, isn't it true that the Singularity represents us all? Isn't that it? We all think what we think, and the voter's watch feeds straight to the Singularity and turns it into one overwhelming opinion, and that means it should be democratically perfect, doesn't it? There's no need for all the lies and politicians, right?" Eddie was taken aback, but quietly considered what Jessop was suggesting. He was more concerned with Jessop's change of demeanor from the calmly weary officer he had gotten used to.

"Now hold on a second Jess, weren't you going on about the Singularity's 'perfect justice' when you brought me in here to please the judge?" Eddie regretted his wording in real time. "Now You wait! Who do you think you're talking to, Ed!? I'm the guard, you're in jail. You need to watch yourself." Eddie cringed through the whole statement, and apologized. Jessop was right, he had been disrespectful for no reason.

"Listen to what I'm saying, Ed. More and more people are being taken in and convicted for things like dissent and protest. More and more of them ... in a perfect democracy? It's bothering me. Shouldn't the number of dissidents be going down and not up? And what about the terrorists? There's always an attack after they are discovered. What do you think?"

"Well, I guess, I agree. I must, I'm here. I think this whole thing is a joke, and we're the butt. We who think differently." Eddie said out loud, and then thought to himself, "We who don't vote alone the lines we're expected to ... who don't vote in the prescribed way," On opening his mouth, he hedged "I don't know, but I do know I'm sitting in jail for not saying something." Eddie consciously lowered his voice back down, "I mean if they can force us to ... hell, if there's a they to point at, even, this is no democracy." Eddie continued. "But it's the way it is, what use is it to worry? What solution can we even start to accomplish? And even if we knew ... What army? Have some dinner and get on with the nap shift. I mean night shift." His joke going unnoticed for a time.

Jessop put on a smile by way of performance, and tried to telegraph the weight dropping from him. It did not drop from him. Paradoxical to his cheery pantomime, he spoke in gray tones. "I think they're hiding us." Jessop let the dramatic gravitas of the sentence fall before he continued, "I think they're hiding people who don't agree, some how. Anyway, there are so many being brought in for deviations, and yet the votes..." he looked as if he had lost himself  for a moment, overwhelmed by the implications of it all, "It doesn't compute, the numbers don't math. Ah hell I don't know.", and he didn't.


Chapter 3
---------

All at once, and on every screen and speaker, an increasingly familiar siren began to blare. On every Voter's Watch, the vote came, "and it would be affirmative" Eddie knew. "Rebel Terrorist Assault! Emergency Powers Legislation required for safety.", repeating over and over again. Each watch lit up with only two options, "Yes", or "No". The question, in matters of law and policy, was always a binary one.

"GRANT EMERGENCY POWERS?" flashed on every screen in the area as the warnings blared, and after only thirty seconds our watches turned green, indicating approval before they could even make direct contact with the watch face; a predicted vote intended to match their fingers and timing. It did not match their fingers or timing. 

Both Jessop and Eddie spoke simultaneously, jumping up to their feet, "but I voted no!!!". Eddie watched Jessop opened up the cell, and both of the other cells despite being empty, and strongly urged Eddie to run, fast.

If Eddie's father had taught him one thing in his childhood, it was that when everyone is running away from something, you should probably also run away. Eddie had always thought that was just cowardice, but as as Jessop ran out of the building, he suddenly understood the wisdom. He calculated faster than anything ever has calculated, and worked out that there was no way to escape the rocket that was presently arriving to disassemble his atomic structure in a jarring way. It disassembled his atomic structure in a most jarring way.

Less than a moment later, or rather at the same time somehow, he and Jessop ran out of the building together. While Eddie didn't understand what had just happened, he was more than ready to run with the derelict guard. "This can't be real", he thought, "or this can, but that couldn't have been. I have to be dead. But I also have to run. Anyway I'm having a stress hallucination." 

Eddie was not having a stress hallucination.

After getting far enough for safety, Jessop and Eddie put their hands up to their foreheads, blocking the sun but looking for all the world as if they were saluting the rocket that was presently exploding the jailhouse. "Jess ... How did you know that was coming?"

"Like I told you Ed ... They're hiding us. There's no resistance fighters, or terrorists. There's no violent attacks against the people," his look of concern became filled with a sense of defeat, "I think it's them, or it I guess, they're just trying to destroy us! It's the ... it's them." Jessop was clearly afraid the watch would hear him, though the Singularity had always assured us that it is fully binary, recording only yes or no, and has no equipment otherwise. Considering that the watch is powered by a simple self-winding spring mechanism, Eddie doubted the need to worry about them hiding any additional tech. "We'd have noticed in other ways, too, and voting security would be shot if other data was collected." Eddie thought soberly.

"You mean they're trying to silence dissidents?" Eddie asked. "No! Well, not really! I mean kind of yes; us real dissidents. But not the ones on the news." Jessop was talking ahead of himself and swerving in and out of sense. He breathed for a moment; tried to calm down a bit. "They are hiding the true wishes of the people. The democratic ... The votes don't actually matter, see the Singularity just wants us to think we're demanding this -- at least I think so. My watch... But the thing is I haven't seen one single violent dissident come into the jail, not one. Where are they? The Singularity is killing off those of us who don't vote the same way the watch does and notice it, and they blame the attacks on the same!!" 

Eddie didn't immediately click onto what Jessop was really saying, his mind still reeling after both seeing the exploding building and being in it. He tried to convince himself it was a terrified hallucination, and so it was, "So, what does that mean Jess? That the attacks are fake? A psychological operation of some kind?" 

Jessop spoke again, with a little more organization. "I knew the attack was incoming the same way you did, Ed. And I don't think you need an explanation for that.", and indeed, he did not. Not with the image of the rocket coming toward his face still filling his shocked mind on a spinning stochastic repeat. Eddie's stomach dropped.


Chapter 4
---------

Days later, while trying to lay low as both Eddie and Jessop independently presumed they were being followed and targeted, they finally arrived at the home of Stacy Rapton, Eddie's sister. "Well; do we just knock and act normal, like some kind of a visit? Of course, why not! Why not tell your sister that the cop who arrested her brother for not talking is now on a regular social call with him," Jessop's mind was running even faster than his mouth.

"No. We're going to tell her what's happening." Eddie muttered in a matter-of-fact way, the way in which he had always spoken. "We're just going to knock on the door, tell her that we died, and also didn't die, and that it's all the Singularity's grand scheme, obviously." his sarcasm so uncomfortably dry as to be invisible from his tone, only made clear by context.

"Well then ... what the hell do we tell her when we get there?" Jessop couldn't handle his uncertainty, and Eddie didn't want to. "Just stand there and look calm. You don't have to say anything. I'll explain everything. That is, I'll explain everything she can handle." Jessop didn't exactly look cheerful, but he was more than happy to shut up and follow in this situation. "Your fatigue must be worse than I thought..." Eddie's mind considered, regarding Jessop's obedience. He felt a nasty sting of acquired responsibility. "You might be a protection client nobody signed on for," he thought at Jessop.

As they reached the little suburban-style ersatz city -- all cities were of course the same city in truth, with the same government and the same culture. "They keep these places all identical, and all perfectly clean. The robots do, and the look is enforced by law" Eddie thought;  the differences were there for cultural enrichment only, and not political necessity as with the older ways of more splintered governance and countries with natural borders rather than resource-coordinated Singular cities. 

"It's this next house, I think." Eddie told Jessop half reassuringly. In Jessop's mind, he was considering the difficulties involved with living in a place like this when every house and street was identical. He wondered about how people made it home when drunk or how they gave directions. His own clave was designed to seem more of a cul-de-sac neighborhood, but it was for a much lower pay grade. Ostensibly, this maintained a healthy spectrum of cultures and economic differences. As Jessop saw it, however,  it maintained the gap between rich and poor castes as a matter of policy.

"I'll be damned if these aren't all the same house", Eddie said in his mind. "The actual same house, with the same people like mirrors, or I'll be ... I really hope she still lives here, it's been a while". Out loud, a much more confident voice could be heard making its way out of his face, "This is it, try not to look too ... cop-ish." He knocked on the door.  

Jessop looked cop-ish.


Chapter 5
---------

"Well, come in then, don't just stand out there being visible!" Eddie's sister Stacy said in the same kind of unpleasant tone the judge had used with him earlier, and a part of him felt like behaving the same way he did then. He thought better of it, given the way his legs felt by this point. "Thanks, we've been running all day." he said, expecting some kind of standard return. "Oh don't thank me, I don't want to be doing this at all," was what came out of her.

"I should tell her about what's happened, I know that, but she'll definitely think I've gone off the deep end, or that we're setting her up for some weird joke," Eddie's mind scrambled, looking for his next words carefully. "Well, you should probably sit down for this." Stacy stood with her arms crossed and looking quite the opposite of amused. Eddie took the hint and just spat it out.

"We've just been killed, but don't worry, we're fine." His mouth moved, and the air from his lungs made it audible as he cringed inside, hearing himself as the wildly ranting man in the tube cars, except the ranting man was paid to be there as part of a misguided effort to make life seem more natural. An effort of course demanded by the people. "Demanded by the people my ass", his mind said, "Or, rather, demanded by the people's ass." He ended his thought by way of repetition with his mouth, "Well anyway we were blown up. Then we weren't, and we ran all the way here."

"You ran directly here?" Stacy angrily jabbed. "They'll have followed you! The scanner sweeps will have seen you. How stupid can you two be? Don't answer that. Just get down to the basement, both of you. You're so lucky I owe you Eddie." Both Eddie and Jessop stayed motionless for one quarter of a second too long, earning an additional assault from Stacy's tongue, "The basement," she growled, "Now." And so they went down. Eddie and Jessop walked down into the basement, and prepared to hide there in the dark, or whatever protection Eddie's sister would offer them.

"You do know you're both batshit insane, right? Coming straight here like this; leading them here." Stacy barked. The two men were startled, and Jessop nearly screamed, "Jesus Christ! How did you even get here. You didn't follow us down." Stacy looked proud of herself, "I didn't follow you down, I have other ways in and out. We have to, because of the scanners." Eddie looked up quickly at her in the dim light, wondering to himself at first, and then aloud "Who does ... who is we? Who else?"

After a little more than an hour of talking with Stacy, what they knew was that they were not alone, and they hadn't been the first to notice about either the votes out of sync, or the missing dissidents. When the light went on, there were actually a number of others in the room who had been practically invisible in the darkness, making themselves now known. "Who are all of these, and ... " Stacy cut Eddie's inquiry off, "They're just like you, and Jessop, and me. We've all seen it, or rather we've all experienced it." Eddie thought to himself, "These people all must be dissidents, or at least pursued by the Singular government and policy enforcers. Why else would they have such refined basement hiding technique?" 

"We've been hiding and meeting down here for months now. We get together and look at our watches when a big vote comes up, only we don't vote. When we just watch and let the timer go, the result still appears to be that we had voted. We noticed it isn't the same, it isn't forcing an affirmative exactly. It's been watching us so closely, it knows our votes before we do. At least, that's what it tries to do." a small woman in a large fluffy jacket and upscale high-fashion with famous names all over spoke in a voice that belied a much more complex person than her clothing would indicate. Jessop immediately had more questions come to mind than he could ask, including her name and number, and Eddie silently considered what he had just heard. Her name, as it would turn out, was Eloise Blane. And her number, she opted not to give him.

Finally, Eddie broke silence after a protracted time. "Well... did you all die too?" Every individual in the room wore an expression different from the next, not a single repeated reaction in the entire group. "Each of them seem to be in their own world right now," he thought to himself. "Maybe they are, actually. Maybe we all are." Without really thinking it through, his mouth proceeded to speak the words for all to hear, "Well, Did each and every one of you die, like me and Jessop? I can see that none of you are surprised. Of all your reactions ...none of you are surprised. None of you. So you're all dead too, like some kind of --" As he thought and reached for his next word, Eloise cut him off in her authoritative but kind voice, "We call ourselves the Martyred Saints," and in likewise fashion, he cut her off as well "as in the saints who wait for their total number to be killed, from the Bible? Is that the idea?" She smiled knowingly, pleased with his awareness of the inspirational material.

Jessop walked toward the center of the room, looking even more nervous than Eddie had gotten used to. He spoke up to everyone in a bold enough voice, he could still stand up with the strongest if he had to. "What we have all been through is, I believe, is called Quantum Immortality. I've been looking into it for a while, since the first time I experienced two things at once like that. If miss Blane will allow, I'd like to explain?" Jessop was being as polite as he possibly could in deferring, and though it cost him an effort to swallow some pride it payed off. "Yes, please, I think I see where you're going. Go on."


Chapter 6
---------

"So you're saying" a voice spoke a little too quietly to be clear, "that the Singularity has been picking off dissidents, and we're being ... well, funneled? Toward the one reality where we all weren't killed. But why?" Jessop confidently explained "Well that's not exactly what the Singularity is trying to accomplish. Its job is to homogenize us, in a way. And it's been doing that by silent coercion. It's timed those votes from our watches with a predicted value, an algorithm about each individual ... it votes for us before we even do it ourselves, but the voter's finger touches the watch at the same exact time." Jessop had clearly studied the subject in depth. "And what it does next is when some of us don't actually press the button we were expected to, when its predictions don't pan out ... well, it kills us. In its own way it thinks it's helping. It's culling away the unpredictable elements; the potentially messy elements like all of you and I, see?" He gave silence for a moment long enough for anyone to speak up, but could only hold it for so long, "But the problem, well you know, you all felt it. Experienced it. But I think Eloise can explain that part better?" He held a hand out to the almost comically fancy woman, inviting her to the center of the room with him.

"Yes," Eloise spoke with a subtle power that seemed counter to her opulent display of wealth outwardly as she walked forward, "Yes I believe I can. While the Singularity is trying to cull the dissidents, it can only do so in one type of reality, and you must understand there are nearly infinite. They just aren't differentiated for us until something kills us -- then we have to branch off to ensure the quantum observer remains grounded." Some of the group seemed to be following, and some not. All of them listened intently nonetheless, "That is to say it can only act on the realities in which it has not itself been destroyed. At the same time, each of us goes on in the realities in which it has not killed us. Because of that political culling process, it's unintentionally concentrating us into a common reality between us. It's trying to cleanse the Singular world of us, of course, but it doesn't actually know what it's doing." 

Showing something resembling excitement on full understanding, Eddie added to the lecture, "It's only trying to do one thing; it's trying to create political union by whatever means necessary. It doesn't know; it isn't that kind of smart, it's just got a goal and tools, and we're in its way." He didn't step forward, and let the statement hang in the dust-specked air until the very meanings of the words grew stale along with it. Nobody in the room fully understood the weight, but all of them had felt the phenomenon.

"I wonder what happens if we all get blown up at the same time," the disturbing thought ran through Eddie's mind as he listened to the others continue on talking. "Would that be a win for the Singularity or for Us? And anyway what are we supposed to do about any of this?" He considered whether to ask that aloud. 

Eddie asked aloud.

"Yes, Eddie, yes, that's just it, isn't it? What do we do about any of this? What even Can we do, and you all must be asking it too." Eloise opened a what seemed a motivational speech, "Well we fight! We can win so long as we can make it to the physical shell of the Singularity itself; the Corpus they call it. Most of us knew people outside the group before being killed, and many of those people work for the Singular government. Some, with any luck, may be willing to help us get close if we can turn them." Eloise spoke in a most comforting tone. 

"Winning hearts and minds. Clearly politically experienced," Eddie thought to himself, "she must have been a higher up in some section of the Singular party." Eddie felt like following her might be the wrong move. 

Eddie did follow her.

"I suppose", Eddie considered silently, "even if she gets us killed, we're just going to keep coming at the Singularity in every reality we aren't dead in yet, die in those too, a get squeezed into one big universe-donut-shaped hole." He felt like he could rally behind the inevitable success implied. He was in, whatever they were going to do.

"I do have a plan", Eloise projected her voice to the room reassuringly, managing to still keep it quiet enough to avoid being a screaming voice in a quiet neighborhood. "Years of teaching in large rooms with thin walls, I suppose" thought Eddie as Eloise went on. "We're going to have to find the Corpus. And we're going to have to physically get to it and enter, which will require help. We can't very well bring everyone for like this, so we're going to need to prioritize by skill set and the rest will follow a beacon after." Eloise directed; everyone was content to follow her lead.

"We're going to need one person who understands computer security, one who can navigate the underground, and one who can rebuild old tools. Who's here with those abilities?" Eloise seemed to have a very clear plan from the direct way she stated the requirements, though she didn't disclose it.

"Jessop, you seemed to understand this whole situation conceptually better than the rest of us, do you have any advantages that your position might grant you?" Jessop thought hard about what his uniform could buy, and he decided time was the answer. "Well I still have my badge and uniform don't I? I can get us into some places; buy us time and make distraction and like that."

Eloise wasn't harsh, but clearly did not enjoy the situation, and Stacy's face mirrored the same gray look, showing that she was listening intently. "That's good. We need a navigator for the underground. We'll be lost without live mapping or GPS, but we can't use them or we'll be seen by the system. We can't use a beacon until we're there." Eddie voiced a warning, "Eventually, the Singularity is going to realize the threat it it and try to clean up the mess. Which is us. It might be more vigilant." 

A tall and thin man stepped forward from the crowd "I used to drive the tunnels. Noticed the vote didn't match what I pressed, and the tunnel I was working blew up in a fireball. Gas I guess ... well anyway you know the rest." 

Eloise smiled at the man graciously, "Thank you Hieronymus! You will be very important in this. Now you, Eddie. Your sister has told me about you, and how you tinker; or that's what she calls it. But the type of trouble you always seem to be in sounds like you're a very smart man. She says you've had the same motorcycle for over 20 years, and that means you can rebuild machines. And if you can't, you're about to learn in a hurry." Eloise stood tall with her hands on her wide hips and her chest puffed, visibly proud of herself. "This will do; this will be our group. This is our bet against the Singularity."

"What about the computer security skill?" Eddie asked, noting the hole in the design of the group. "I have that one myself Mr. Rapton, I would rather have someone better but it looks like I will have to do." She seemed about as confident and serious as a woman in a giant ridiculous fluffy jacket reasonably could, Eddie considered. "Well, I don't see anyone else with a plan," he thought his words more deliberately, "So I guess this is what we're doing." He then said aloud, "To the tubes then ... I suppose?" 

They went down the hole, and to the tubes.


Chapter 7
---------

The group went down a manhole within the basement, which the house appeared to have been built over. "Well, that's not to code", thought Eddie, his imagination thrown back momentarily to his few, unpleasant, clave construction gigs. Early on in the Singular integration.

"While we're down here, Eddie, explain to us what you know about pre-perception." Eloise spoke as a teacher speaks, "What I know about what?" Eddie was a little flustered, and felt like she was deliberately to embarrass him. "Well you ride a motorcycle, right?" she asked, and it shined on him what she meant.

"I see, yes, pre-perception. What that means, then, is that there's always more than one possible outcome, and you have to be ready for all the likely ones, whatever they are." Eddie let a silence pass, and saw the look of expectation, "I guess I've got to keep going then," he thought. And he spoke, "You've got to get through the corner haven't you? But you see the worst and the at the same time, so your reaction time is cut out no matter which one happens. Is that what you're getting at?" Eddie explained perfectly. 

"Yes, exactly that! But the theory goes that perceiving the most likely next moment's possibilities isn't what it seems. When one out of three possibilities might kill you; that one happens too. All of them occur in superposition, the events remaining undistinguished until they diverge; and you stop living in the universe where it did. But you yourself go on in the others." She didn't sound cheerful about it, but did seem pleased at the understanding. "Well, I guess there's no other choice, and what's the worst that could happen?", thought Eddie.

They walked for what felt to Eddie like an interminably long distance down a dark tunnel that wasn't intended to be walked,before eventually seeing light. A small tube station, complete with cars and an interchange. "I've never been in the tubes in this area", Jessop commented, nervous over the silence as much as anything else. "Where I'm from, there's just the one lane both ways and you've got to bring your own car." He looked around, overwhelmed at the complexity. Hieronymus gave a reassurance, "I've been driving these tubes since I could drive sir, I've got this." And before he was done speaking, Eddie had finished preparing one of the near-by public cars for them to take. "Alright then, get in and show us." Eddie said with a slight hint, just the slightest, of pride.

The car, an older model that seemed to be barely running an held together with nothing but blind hope, was maintaining its speed through the superconducting magnet assisted corners, giving a sensation that one's stomach was experiencing lag while the rest of the body proceeded along with the car. "This car might not be nice, but it's also so crap that it doesn't have spying and reporting tech like navigation. Doesn't even have an radio VIN signal tag. They couldn't run it if they tried," Eddie was proud of his good choice and solid reasoning for it. "If the watches can't do it, this clunk can't either." he hoped. "Or else, we're pretty well-stewed."

Hieronymus was deciding paths before anyone else even knew what was coming up, going too fast to really see anything at all. He'd been a cabbie in the tubes all across the Singular country and some of the larger cities too, for so long his brain had devoted an entire new structure to nothing but that job, and it had become perfected. He stayed silent, but listened to Eloise directing from clave to clave the whole time, focusing in at least three directions at all times. "I don't get it", Eddie protested, "Why did you have me explain pre-perception instead of Hieronymus?". Eloise smiled and answered, "Hieronymus is shy." In his mind, Eddie cursed.

"How do you know where we're going miss Blane?" Jessop asked in his compellingly respectful way; he'd honed the skill in trying to get by without much friction in his work. "I don't!" she smirked in the way one does when they are aware of an inappropriate smile coming to their face and are trying to stifle it like a poorly timed sneeze, "but I do know where we can find out." Jessop felt uneasy about adding yet another person to a group trying to avoid detection. 

"Are you sure about not being tracked, Ed?" Jessop asked, his stomach refusing to settle as the magnetic corner fought Newton with all its might. "I'm sure of it. They could track them all back in the corporate mass surveillance days because they used cameras and cellular signals. These days it's all self-snitching cars, so they don't use the expensive infrastructure system anymore." Eddie tried to help Jessop relax with some good information. It didn't make Jessop feel better.

Jessop didn't feel better when they arrived at the last tube interchange and exit, and he didn't feel better when the surface turned out to be what look like the exact same clave Stacy's house was in. He didn't feel better when they walked into a house duplicate to Stacy's house. He didn't feel better when the nervous man inside said a meek, "I'm sorry Eloise." Chapter 8

Eddie asked, and Jessop's face showed clearly that he did not. "What's going on here," Jessop demanded of the full room, "what are you doing?"

The policy officers closed in and cuffed them all, thanking the man in the chair for the help. Eloise made such an uncharacteristic harsh sound in a charmingly incompetent chastisement, "You filthy stupid cabbage-eyed snake! Don't you know you're still an anomaly? They're still going to take you in, fool!!"

Eddie saw some distinct possibilities in his mind, or his imagination, or the universal quantum superposition time-space, or wherever it is possibilities live. 

Out loud, his question was simpler, "Are you arresting us, or detaining us?" This, he felt, would answer which was to happen. "We're doing our job sir. Now shut your mouth and get moving." the guard holding his cuffs pushed, refusing to give any meaningful information. "Free speech my ass," Eddie quipped as the officer put him into a surface car.

"Well, I suppose we aren't going to need to locate the Corpus if that's where they take us," Eddie's mind wandered, until it struck him more sharply what Jessop had said before. "Wait. They're taking us in and they'll call us terrorists. Violent rebels. Attackers. But Jessop said he'd never seen a violent rebel actually in the jail. That means they aren't planning to take us to any jail; they're going to hide us the way they hid the others who never turn up again after arrest," He struggled to keep his mouth closed, "We either get funneled again as supposed terrorists, get funneled by supposed terrorists, or else we get taken to the Singularity's Corpus itself by these guys," he thought to himself. 

Jessop however, in a different car, did not keep his mouth closed for more than a second. Instead he used the opportunity to demand, beg, cajole; he tried to persuade the policy officers to stop, to think reasonably about it. "Look, I'm one of you! He lost coherence, his words coming out in scrambles the officers easily ignored, and went silent as Hieronymus kicked him from the other seat, glaring at the idiotically panicked Jessop. "Shut up, stupid. These guys aren't like you." He was right of course. Jessop remained silent for the rest of the drive, listening to the radio traffic and its near complete lack of crime reports despite the claims of terrorism. He spent the whole time trying to piece together what was really happening, while biting his tongue continuously, harder and harder.

Eddie watched out the window opposite Eloise, silently, as the car came to a stop in an interchange and the two policy officers went got out and walked toward another officer's car. "I can see a little into that car... they have Hieronymus and Jessop over there." he thought. He also said it, and Eloise agreed. "It looks like they're going over to talk to the other officers. Maybe they don't know what to do with us?" After a short time, the two officers came back. They opened the doors and ordered Eloise and Eddie out of the car, taking off the cuffs. "This is a blind spot. It won't know." The officer unshackling Eddie said in a hushed voice. He decided it would be best to just take in the information, rather than responding in any way. He watched over his shoulder as the officers from the other car did the same for Hieronymus and Jessop.

The officers got back in the cars without saying anything more, and drove away, and the group came together. "How are we supposed to know where we are now?" Asked Eddie, and Eloise answered. "We can trust our Hero to have paid attention the whole time, I would bet on it," she said in a hopeful tone. Hieronymus grinned, his eyes seeming almost vacant but his mind sharp. Hieronymus nodded confirmation and beckoning at the same time silently, and walked away with all the quiet confidence, and the others knew to follow. Down two or three hundred meters of tubes and countless near misses from cars, the Saints arrived at another interchange identical to every other interchange; they were all identical. "Without the navigation system in a car, there is no way to know what street one is on or where in the country. Hieronymus was not like the rest of them.

It's part of the gentrification simulating initiative." Hieronymus commented in explanation. "That initiative..." Eddie thought, "it doesn't sound like something we the people would have democratically chosen". He was very certain everyone had that thought, so he did not speak it.

After choosing a car, Hieronymus mentioned what should have been obvious to them all, "We can't just drive to Elmont with any car, they'll see where we are the whole time remember?" Why not keep us all in different jails? I don't think it does want us all together. I think there are more of us Martyred Saints there. I think those officers might be the good guys." Eloise and Jessop simultaneously confirmed in their own distinct mannerisms. Eloise added "If it gets us all at once, and in every universe, that could actually end us.
